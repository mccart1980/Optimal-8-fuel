**Why ~0.7 kg a month and not more.** Muscle has a rate limit. A bigger surplus doesn't beat it — it adds fat, which is mass that enters the punch without producing force and costs you in round five. The tape is the referee: every four to six weeks, arms, shoulders, waist. If the arms and shoulders are moving and the waist isn't, the food is right, whatever the scale says.

## WHEN BOXING RETURNS — THE FOOD RULE FOR CAMP MODE

The training load comes back, so the food does too. One line: **on every boxing day that doesn't already have a 5pm feed, add a CARB TOP-UP at 17:30 — low fat, low fibre, easy to move on — and push dinner to 20:15.** That lifts the weekly average to about 3,800 — carbs, not protein. Sparring day gets the same top-up plus a banana and electrolytes at 18:45. The bike sessions and the fight sim leave the week in camp mode; their shakes go with them. Nothing else changes.

## EASY WEEKS AND THE TAPER

**Easy weeks (5 and 10): keep eating exactly as written.** The training drops; the building doesn't. Recovery weeks are where the adaptation lands, the surplus is only ~190 to begin with, and the tape is the referee. The only change: no fight rounds means no mid-session banana on Sunday.

**Taper (weeks 15–16): do not cut carbs.** The classic mistake is eating less because you're training less — and arriving at test day with an empty tank. Volume drops, food holds, and you walk into the Saturday of week 16 full. Test day eats exactly like a normal Saturday.

## THE BATCH COOK — TWO PANS, WEIGHED ONCE

Fourteen portions a week: thirteen standard, one big. Cook Sunday, cook Wednesday, half a batch each time.

**The two pans.** The mince pan: the mince browned, the passata and beef stock in, simmered down — one pan, everything in it. The sweet potato: roasted or boiled, separately. They are cooked apart because they cook at different rates and lose different amounts of water, so one pot weighed together tells you nothing about either.

**Weigh each pan once, cooked, in the app.** The COOK tab has two calculators. Put in the portions you cooked — standard and big — then the cooked weight of the mince pan (minus the pan) and the cooked weight of the sweet potato. It gives you two numbers: cooked grams of mince per portion, cooked grams of sweet potato per portion, standard and big. Those two numbers then show on every batch card in the day plans, and you serve by them for good — until the recipe or the pan changes, when you weigh once more.

**The maths, so you trust it.** Raw, a standard portion is 150 g mince and 150 g passata in the mince pan, and 300 g of sweet potato; a big portion is 200 g and 150 g, and 350 g. So a standard portion's share of the cooked mince pan is 300 parts, a big portion's 350; of the sweet potato, 300 and 350. Worked example, full batch of 12 standard and 2 big: the mince pan started at 4,300 g raw; if it weighs 3,500 g cooked, a standard portion is 3,500 × 300 ÷ 4,300 = 244 g and a big one 285 g. The sweet potato started at 4,300 g raw; roasted to 3,400 g, a standard portion is 237 g, a big one 277 g. Your pans will give different numbers. That's why you weigh.

**Add 150 g mushrooms per portion** — 33 kcal and one of only two vegetables you eat.

## THE HONEST GAP

You eat sweet potato and mushrooms and no other vegetables. I'm not spending a page on broccoli — a plan you won't follow is worth nothing — but the numbers are properly counted:

**Fibre:** ~30–35 g a day from the sweet potato, bananas, passata and the sachets. Above the guideline; the sachets carry less than the oats did, and it's still enough. **Vitamin C:** ~150–200 mg a day from sweet potato, bananas and passata. Covered. **Potassium:** excellent. **The real holes are three:** vitamin D in winter, omega-3, and possibly magnesium — the shop list closes all three. **Sugar:** the sachets and the honey add about 40 g of sugar a day over the old porridge; around a training load like this that's fuel, not a problem, and the tape will say so. **Sodium:** you sweat for a living. Salt your food, keep the daily electrolytes, and hold the urine check: pale straw. Dark at 10am means the morning session ran under-watered — drink before bed, not just at 3am.

## AROUND THE SESSION

**Before:** the meal that fuels a session is hours out, not minutes out. For the 3:30 sessions, that's last night's dinner plus the shake twenty minutes before. For Wednesday's heavy morning, Tuesday's 5pm feed as well. For the weekend sessions, the 5pm feed the evening before — Friday loads Saturday, Saturday loads Sunday.

**During:** water and electrolytes everywhere, with one exception — fight-round Sundays get the banana between the throws and the Nordics.

**After:** the window is wider than it was once sold as — but Monday to Thursday you go straight from the gym to a scaffold, and the meal you skip because you're rushing is the one that costs you the muscle. Thermos of porridge. No decisions at 4:50am.

**The easy hour:** water. It's active recovery, not a session, and the day's feeds don't move for it.

## THE FEEDBACK LOOP

Tape every four to six weeks. Waist is the number that decides.

| What's happening | What to do |
|---|---|
| Arms and shoulders up, waist flat | Correct. Change nothing. |
| Waist climbing faster than arms and shoulders | Cut 100–150 kcal — from the 3pm shake on Monday and Thursday. |
| Nothing moving in six weeks, waist flat | Add 200 kcal — one extra CARB TOP-UP. |
| Bodyweight falling, sessions unchanged | You're under-eating. The answer is food, not a program change. |

And name the ceiling deliberately: at 5'8" and 78–82 kg you already carry real mass. Somewhere ahead is a weight where you punch harder and fade sooner. That's a decision to make on purpose, not something to drift into.

## THE COACHING NOTE

Three things carry this, and none of them are a macro.

**The three 5pm feeds.** Tuesday loads Wednesday's trap bar; Friday loads Saturday; Saturday loads Sunday. Three feeds decide the three sessions that decide the program.

**The shake, Monday to Thursday.** Power doses and intervals before a shift. An empty tank at half past three is the cheapest mistake to make and the most expensive one to keep making.

**Cycling the calories.** Saturday 4,148, Monday 3,327 — same week, eight hundred apart. Small enough to stay lean, big enough to build. When boxing returns, the food returns with it — one rule, one line.

Eat for it. The training only writes the cheque.
