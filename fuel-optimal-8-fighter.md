**About that maintenance number.** The old document said 3,400. That under-counted the day job. Eight hours up a scaffold is not desk work with a walk at lunch: for a heavy trade it adds somewhere between 800 and 1,200 kcal over sedentary, and on top of that you train about seven hours a week. Add it up honestly and maintenance lands nearer 3,600. Every one of these numbers carries an error bar of ten to fifteen per cent, which is why no formula gets the last word.

**The tape gets the last word.** A ~280 surplus is at the top of the useful range, not over it — but it is the top. Muscle has a rate limit; past it you are buying fat, and fat is mass that enters the punch without producing force and costs you in round five. Every four to six weeks: arms, shoulders, waist. Arms and shoulders moving, waist flat — the food is right, whatever the scale says. Waist climbing faster — use the trim lever in THE FEEDBACK LOOP, it's already written for you.

## THE THING FOOD CANNOT FIX

Read this one twice, because it is the real ceiling on this program and no amount of eating gets round it.

You are up at about ten to three, training at half three, at work at quarter past five. If you are in bed at half nine you are getting a little over five hours, four nights a week. Muscle is not built in the gym; the gym is the signal. It is built in the hours afterwards, and the biggest single block of those hours is sleep. Five hours is not enough of them.

Nothing on this page fixes that. What the food can do is three things, and it now does all three: protein close to sleep so the short window is at least a fed one, no long empty stretches in the working day, and enough carbohydrate that the next 3:30 start isn't run on fumes. That's the whole of nutrition's contribution to the problem.

The rest is scheduling, and it is worth more than any change I could make to this document: **protect Friday**. Friday is the one morning you sleep, and it is doing more for your arms and shoulders than the 5pm carb feed is. If a week ever has to lose a session, lose one of the four early mornings before you lose Friday. And if you can find an hour on a weekend afternoon to sleep rather than fill, take it — the easy hour is optional, the sleep isn't.

## WHEN BOXING RETURNS — THE FOOD RULE FOR CAMP MODE

The training load comes back, so the food does too. One line: **on every boxing day that doesn't already have a 5pm feed, add a CARB TOP-UP at 17:30 — low fat, low fibre, easy to move on — and push dinner to 20:15.** That lifts the weekly average to about 4,050 — carbs, not protein. Sparring day gets the same top-up plus a banana and electrolytes at 18:45. The bike sessions and the fight sim leave the week in camp mode; their shakes go with them. Nothing else changes.

## EASY WEEKS AND THE TAPER

**Easy weeks (5 and 10): keep eating exactly as written.** The training drops; the building doesn't. Recovery weeks are where the adaptation lands, the surplus isn't large to begin with, and the tape is the referee. The only change: no fight rounds means no mid-session banana on Sunday.

**Taper (weeks 15–16): do not cut carbs.** The classic mistake is eating less because you're training less — and arriving at test day with an empty tank. Volume drops, food holds, and you walk into the Saturday of week 16 full. Test day eats exactly like a normal Saturday.

## THE BATCH COOK — TWO PANS, WEIGHED ONCE

Fourteen portions a week: thirteen standard, one big. Cook Sunday, cook Wednesday, half a batch each time. Boil ten eggs while you're at it — that's the week's snacks done in the same hour.

**The two pans.** The mince pan: the mince browned, the passata and beef stock in, simmered down — one pan, everything in it. The sweet potato: roasted or boiled, separately. They are cooked apart because they cook at different rates and lose different amounts of water, so one pot weighed together tells you nothing about either.

**Weigh each pan once, cooked, in the app.** The COOK tab has two calculators and they don't talk to each other. Each wants two numbers: what went in raw, and what came out cooked. Type them and you get the cooked grams of that pan in one standard portion and one big one. Those numbers then show on every batch card in the day plans, and you serve by them for good — until the recipe or the pan changes, when you weigh once more.

**The maths, so you trust it.** A standard portion is 150 g of raw mince and 300 g of raw sweet potato; a big one is 200 g and 350 g. The app scales that raw share by however much water your pan lost. Worked example: you brown 2,200 g of raw mince and the finished pot — mince, passata and stock, minus the pot — weighs 3,500 g. A standard portion is 3,500 × 150 ÷ 2,200 = 240 g of that pot, and a big one 320 g. You roast 4,300 g of raw sweet potato down to 3,400 g: a standard portion is 235 g, a big one 275 g. Your pans will give different numbers. That's why you weigh.

**Add 150 g mushrooms per portion** — 33 kcal and one of only two vegetables you eat.

## THE HONEST GAP

You eat sweet potato and mushrooms and no other vegetables. I'm not spending a page on broccoli — a plan you won't follow is worth nothing — but the numbers are properly counted:

**Fibre:** ~30–35 g a day from the sweet potato, bananas, passata and the sachets. Above the guideline. **Vitamin C:** ~150–200 mg a day from sweet potato, bananas and passata. Covered. **Potassium:** excellent, and better again with the extra banana. **The extra ten eggs a week** quietly close more of the gap than they look like they should: choline, selenium, and a little vitamin D you weren't getting anywhere else. **The real holes are three:** vitamin D in winter, omega-3, and possibly magnesium — the shop list closes all three. **Sugar:** the sachets and the honey add about 40 g of sugar a day over the old porridge; around a training load like this that's fuel, not a problem, and the tape will say so. **Sodium:** you sweat for a living. Salt your food, keep the daily electrolytes, and hold the urine check: pale straw. Dark at 10am means the morning session ran under-watered — drink before bed, not just at 3am.

## AROUND THE SESSION

**Before:** the meal that fuels a session is hours out, not minutes out. For the 3:30 sessions, that's last night's dinner plus the shake twenty minutes before. For Wednesday's heavy morning, Tuesday's 5pm feed as well. For the weekend sessions, the 5pm feed the evening before — Friday loads Saturday, Saturday loads Sunday.

**During:** water and electrolytes everywhere, with one exception — fight-round Sundays get the banana between the throws and the Nordics.

**After:** the window is wider than it was once sold as — but Monday to Thursday you go straight from the gym to a scaffold, and the meal you skip because you're rushing is the one that costs you the muscle. Thermos of porridge at 04:50, eggs and a banana at 07:00. Between them they put 30 g of protein and 133 g of carbohydrate into the three hours after the session, which is what that session was for. No decisions at 4:50am.

**Before bed:** the NIGHT SHAKE, seven nights. It is the smallest feed in the document and the one people talk themselves out of first. Don't — see THE THING FOOD CANNOT FIX for why it earns its place on your sleep schedule specifically.

**The easy hour:** water. It's active recovery, not a session, and the day's feeds don't move for it.

## THE FEEDBACK LOOP

Tape every four to six weeks. Waist is the number that decides.

| What's happening | What to do |
|---|---|
| Arms and shoulders up, waist flat | Correct. Change nothing. |
| Waist climbing faster than arms and shoulders | Pull the trim lever below. |
| Nothing moving in six weeks, waist flat | Add 200 kcal — one extra CARB TOP-UP. |
| Bodyweight falling, sessions unchanged | You're under-eating. The answer is food, not a program change. |

**The trim lever, in order.** Don't guess at this — take it in this order and stop as soon as the waist settles. First, drop the 15:00 SHAKE + FRUIT to one banana instead of two: −105 kcal a day, and it's the feed with the least work to do. Second, take the banana out of the 07:00 snack and go eggs only: another −105. That's ~3,670 a day, back to roughly a 70 kcal surplus, and you've lost nothing that was doing a job. If the waist is still climbing after both, the problem isn't the food — it's the sleep.

And name the ceiling deliberately: at 5'8" and 78–82 kg you already carry real mass. Somewhere ahead is a weight where you punch harder and fade sooner. That's a decision to make on purpose, not something to drift into.

## THE COACHING NOTE

Four things carry this, and none of them are a macro.

**The three 5pm feeds.** Tuesday loads Wednesday's trap bar; Friday loads Saturday; Saturday loads Sunday. Three feeds decide the three sessions that decide the program.

**The shake, Monday to Thursday.** Power doses and intervals before a shift. An empty tank at half past three is the cheapest mistake to make and the most expensive one to keep making.

**The two feeds nobody notices.** Eggs and a banana at seven, a UFIT at half nine. Between them they cover the two holes this plan used to have — the four-hour morning and the overnight — and they are the two most likely to get skipped because neither feels like a meal. They are the difference between training four early mornings a week and being paid for training four early mornings a week.

**Cycling the calories.** Saturday 4,328, Monday 3,602 — same week, seven hundred apart. Small enough to stay lean, big enough to build. When boxing returns, the food returns with it — one rule, one line.

Eat for it. The training only writes the cheque.
