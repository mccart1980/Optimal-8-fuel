# FUEL — MENU B · ON THE MOVE, AND THE HYDRATION SCHEDULE

**The same plan, eaten standing up. Every feed on Menu B matches the feed it replaces on Menu A to within fifty calories, so you pick A or B meal by meal — sit down when you can, eat walking when you can't — and the day's numbers hold either way.**

## MENU A, CHECKED AGAINST THE CAMP

The camp changes when you train, not what you eat, so Menu A stands as written. It was checked feed by feed against the camp's week: the 5pm top-ups still sit the evening before the three sessions that decide the camp — Tuesday for Wednesday's heavy morning, Friday for Saturday, Saturday for Sunday's rounds; the porridge-big mornings still land on the two heaviest days; Sunday's mid-session banana moves to the gap before the Nordics, as the camp page says; the sauna weeks need nothing extra but water and the electrolytes; and the easy week and the taper eat exactly as written. Two things to hold onto in camp: on the seven-round weeks the Sunday banana is not optional, and nothing on either menu is allowed to slide because of appetite — the tape is the referee and the food is a rule.

## WHY THESE FOODS AND NOT OTHERS

Menu B has three new foods — wraps, bagels and tuna — and adds eggs to the day. Each earns its place. **Wholemeal wraps** carry the batch mince with the sweet potato's carbohydrate and most of its fibre, in a form you can eat one-handed. **Bagels** are dense, dry, unbreakable carbohydrate — the most calories per gram of bread there is, which is exactly what a 5pm top-up or a 3pm feed needs in a bag on a scaffold. **Tinned tuna** is the cleanest portable protein that needs no fridge: 24 grams a tin, no fat to speak of. **Boiled eggs** are the other one — a week in the shell, protein and fat together, and they turn a bagel into a meal. Nothing else was added because nothing else needed to be: the plan's protein was already high, so the new foods carry carbohydrate first and protein second, in the ratio the day was already built on.

Three honest notes. Tuna's omega-3 doesn't survive canning in spring water, so it doesn't replace the fish oil — keep taking it. Tuna is fine at a tin a day for a man your size, but hold it to four a week anyway; the eggs and the mince cover the rest, and variety is its own insurance. And Menu B loses the sweet potato, which is your main source of vitamin C and potassium alongside the bananas — so run Menu A at least half the week, and on a full Menu B day, eat the third banana.

## THE MENU B BLOCKS

**MINCE WRAPS + BANANA · 580 kcal · P42 · C73 · F11.** Two standard wholemeal tortilla wraps (about 40 g each). One standard portion of the pot mince — the cooked-mince number from the app's calculator — split between the two, rolled tight, wrapped in foil. A banana alongside. The mince is the same mince, from the same pot; the wraps stand in for the sweet potato. Made the night before, cold, from the fridge.

**TUNA & EGG BAGEL + BANANA · 590 kcal · P47 · C72 · F12.** One plain bagel, one tin of tuna in spring water drained, two boiled eggs sliced onto it. Banana alongside. Assembled the night before and foiled, or carried as parts — bagel, tin, eggs — and put together at the break. Salt and pepper, nothing else needed.

**EGG BAGEL + BANANA · 568 kcal · P30 · C72 · F17.** One plain bagel, three boiled eggs, a banana. The lightest on protein of the three and the easiest to carry — nothing to open.

**ONE BAGEL · 230 kcal · P9 · C45 · F1.** The 3pm feed, when two bananas are two things too many to carry. Honey on it if you want; it's 60 calories you'll use.

**BAGEL TOP-UP · 521 kcal · P18 · C106 · F2.** Two bagels with 20 g of honey between them. The 5pm feed on a night you're not home by five — same carbohydrate as the pouch, no fridge, no microwave. It loads tomorrow's session exactly as the pouch does.

**BIG portions on the weekend:** three wraps instead of two, or a second egg on the tuna bagel. Fifty calories either way; the tape decides the rest.

## THE PREP — ONCE, TWICE A WEEK

On the batch days — Sunday and Wednesday — three extra jobs, fifteen minutes:

1. **Portion the mince separately.** The pot calculator already gives you the cooked grams per portion. Put four standard portions of mince alone into tubs for the wraps; the rest goes into the batch tubs with the sweet potato as now.
2. **Boil the eggs.** Twelve on Sunday, nine on Wednesday, ten minutes from boiling, straight into cold water. In the shell they keep a week in the fridge; peel them the night before, not the week before.
3. **Bagels and tins in the bag.** Bagels keep a week; tuna keeps forever. Two bagels and a tin live in the work bag permanently, so a day that goes wrong still has a Menu B in it.

**The night before:** wraps rolled and foiled, eggs peeled into a tub, bananas counted. **Cold mince is cold mince:** in a cool bag with an ice block from the fridge to the break, and eaten by lunchtime. Wraps made at 9pm and eaten at 12:30 with no cooling in between is the one way Menu B goes wrong; a £6 cool bag and a freezer block fix it for good.

## HYDRATION — WHEN TO DRINK, AND WHAT

Thirst is a bad guide for anyone who sweats for a living, and right now it's a worse one, because what you're running blunts it. So the schedule replaces thirst: fixed times, fixed amounts, set as alarms, ticked off. The target is about 4½ litres on a work day and 4 on a weekend — a body your size doing a physical job and a hard session needs roughly 3 litres just to stand still and another litre or two for the sweat — and the two urine checks are the referee: pale straw at 10am and 4pm, or the next alarm's amount goes up.

**Kit:** one 1-litre bottle for work, one 500 ml bottle for the gym, electrolyte sachets in the bag. Two sachets a day is the ceiling — three only on a sauna day — and salt your food; past that it's salt water and nothing else.

### WORK DAYS — set these alarms

### FRIDAY — the sleep day

No session, so drop the session bottle and the porridge drink and run the work-day schedule from the work-start alarm. About 4 litres.

### THE FOUR RULES

1. **The 5:15 litre with the sachet is the one that never moves.** You start every shift already sweating from a session; this is what stops the day going dark at ten.
2. **Pale straw at 10 and 4.** The check outranks the schedule — dark means more, however much you've drunk.
3. **Cramps at night, a headache in the afternoon, a resting heart rate up with no other reason:** under-replaced yesterday. Sachet 2 today, salt on dinner, and the 14:00 goes to 500.
4. **Nothing big after eight.** The camp runs on sleep.

## THE COACHING NOTE

Menu B exists for one reason: a feed eaten standing up beats a feed not eaten. The plan was built on six feeds a day, and a scaffold doesn't always give you a seat for them; this makes sure the seat was never the point. Same numbers, same timings, same rule — the 5pm top-ups never move, whichever bread they come on — and the tape decides whether it's working. Sit when you can. Eat when you can't.
